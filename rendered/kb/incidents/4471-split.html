Where each one stands

From the engineering update, #4471 closed 5 October:

  • parser: fixed. fix(dates): parse dd/mm for eu locales. Ticket closed.
  • hint: won’t fix. The booking-v3 form is retired, so its hint goes with it. Ticket closed.
  • shift: open. The update does not mention it. A dd/mm parser and a retired form leave a timezone conversion where it was.

The problems

parser: the shared parser reads dates month first. Every tenant writes the day first, so 02/12 (2 December) comes out as 12 February. Every tenant, every way in. Fixed on 5 October.

hint: the booking-v3 form tells people to type the month first. It prints MM/DD/YYYY under its date field, then reads the date the tenant’s way. People do what the hint says and the date flips. Only reports through the form of a booking-v3 tenant. The parser fix does not touch it: the hint has to change.

shift: the date moves by one day. Not a swap: Tuesday 12 May shows as Monday 11 May, a client in Lisbon gets Tuesday while one in Toronto gets Monday, an invite arrives before the booking. That is a timezone conversion, not the parser and not the form. The desk had already read these as a one-day move (desk-notes.json) but filed them under parser by tenant.

How this was counted

  • One id, one report: the export pages overlap, so repeats are dropped before counting.
  • The cause comes from the tenant and the way in (#lib/kb/causes.ts), not from the intake tag. Intake guesses from the reporter’s browser, and the guess is often wrong.
  • Causes are counted the desk’s way, as the export’s own view counts them. The one-day moves from the desk’s notes get a row and a ticket of their own, and stay counted under the cause they are filed in.
  • Every number on this page is computed from the export when the page renders. When the desk merges more reports, the table follows.

Why it was one ticket

Decision 0001 merges reports whose subject lines match, without reading the body. Every report here has a subject like “Date is wrong”, so different bugs ended up in one thread. Its own consequences say engineering would split them later; this page is that split.
